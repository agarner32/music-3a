G Natural Minor
Natural

To find G natural minor, count up 3 half steps from G — that lands on B♭. B♭ major has two flats: B♭ and E♭. G natural minor uses those same two flats, starting and ending on G. The pattern is W–H–W–W–H–W–W.

G Harmonic Minor
Harmonic

Start with G natural minor and raise the 7th degree by a half step. F♮ becomes F♯ — creating a strong leading tone that pulls back to G. This produces an augmented 2nd between E♭ (6th) and F♯ (7th) — the exotic, distinctive sound of harmonic minor.

⚠️ The 7th degree won't always gain a sharp. It depends on the key — sometimes it gains a natural sign (♮) or even a double sharp (𝄪). Always think "raise by one half step" rather than "add a sharp."

G Melodic Minor
Melodic

Start with G natural minor and raise both the 6th and 7th degrees ascending (E♭→E♮, F♮→F♯). This smooths out the awkward augmented 2nd. Descending, lower them back — returning to natural minor on the way down.

Natural

E Harmonic Minor
Harmonic

Start with E natural minor and raise the 7th degree by a half step. D♮ becomes D♯ — creating the leading tone that pulls strongly back to E. Notice the augmented 2nd between C♮ (6th) and D♯ (7th).

⚠️ Here the 7th gains a sharp — but that's specific to this key. Always think "raise by one half step" and let the accidental follow from there.
