Roman Numeral Detective

Work through four examples step by step — finding the root, identifying the scale degree, and naming the Roman numeral.

Key of A major — G♯ B E
Major Key — First Inversion
1
The key signature has 3 sharps (F♯, C♯, G♯) → A major. The chord tones are G♯, B, and E. The bass note is G♯. The notes are not in thirds order so this is an inversion — we need to find the root.
2
Test the combinations starting from the bottom note G. Remember: accidentals don't affect the letter name.
✗
G B D? — No. Our chord has E, not D.
✗
B D F? — No. Our chord has G and E, not D and F.
✓
E G B? — Yes. The root is E.
3
E is scale degree 5 in A major → Roman numeral V. V is major. The G♯ confirms the major quality.
4
The bass note G♯ is the 3rd of the E major triad. 3rd in the bass = first inversion → V⁶.

Key of D major — A D F♯
Major Key — Second Inversion
1
The key signature has 2 sharps (F♯, C♯) → D major. The chord tones are A, D, F♯. The bass note is A. Not in thirds order — find the root.
2
Test combinations starting from the bottom note A.
✗
A C E? — No. Our chord has D and F, not C and E.
✗
C E G? — No. Our chord has A, D, and F.
✓
D F A? — Yes. D is D, F♯ is still an F, A is A. The root is D.
3
D is scale degree 1 in D major → Roman numeral I. I is major. F♯ confirms the major quality.
4
The bass note A is the 5th of the D major triad. 5th in the bass = second inversion → I⁶/⁴.

Key of E minor — F♯ A C
Minor Key
1
The key signature has 1 sharp (F♯) → E minor (relative of G major). The chord tones are F♯, A, C. The notes are in thirds order — root position. The bottom note F♯ is the root.
2
Since it's in root position, no cycling needed. Confirm the combination: F A C — yes. Root is F♯.
3
F♯ is scale degree 2 in E minor → Roman numeral ii. In natural minor, scale degree 2 is diminished.
4
F♯–A is a minor 3rd, A–C is a minor 3rd, F♯–C is a diminished 5th. Two minor thirds = diminished → ii°. Root position — no figured bass needed.

Key of G minor — A D F♯
Minor Key — Accidental
1
The key signature has 2 flats (B♭, E♭) → B♭ major or G minor. The chord contains F♯ — an accidental not found in either key signature.
2
Accidental clue: F♯ does not exist in B♭ major. An accidental like this almost always signals a minor key — the raised 7th from harmonic minor. The key is G minor.
3
The chord tones are A, D, F♯. The bass note is A. Not in thirds order — find the root. Test combinations starting from A.
✗
A C E? — No. Our chord has D and F, not C and E.
✗
F A C? — No. Our chord has D, not C.
✓
D F A? — Yes. D is D, F♯ is still an F, A is A. The root is D.
4
D is scale degree 5 in G minor → Roman numeral V. The F♯ (raised 7th) makes this a major triad — the standard dominant in minor.
5
The bass note A is the 5th of the D major triad. 5th in the bass = second inversion → V⁶/⁴.
